Very-long-term conservation

A new paradigm of ex situ conservation, resting on the ultra-drying of seeds and their sealing in hermetic containers.

New paradigm of ex situ conservation

This pioneering bank inaugurated a new paradigm of ex situ conservation, differing from existing cultivated-seed banks in three respects: the type of material conserved, the methods and places of collection, and the conservation method. Since wild germplasm is far more diverse and poorly known than cultivated germplasm, ex situ conservation had to change paradigm, reorienting itself toward very-long-term conservation, resting on two pillars: the ultra-drying of seeds and their storage in highly hermetic containers to prevent rehydration.

Ultra-drying

A technique developed for the very-long-term conservation of wild seeds, reducing their moisture content well below the levels typical of cultivated-seed banks.

Hermetic containers

After testing many types of container, the one guaranteeing the greatest hermeticity was selected: the flame-sealed glass tube. Inside the tube, alongside the seeds, a humidity indicator is placed. This method of conservation, although more laborious than the standard methods of the time, guarantees the sought-after very-long-term conservation.

Safety duplicates

In 1980, coinciding with the development of seed banks in Spain, BGV-UPM began a plan to create safety duplicates: it sends and receives duplicates from other Spanish banks, chiefly the Córdoba Germplasm Bank, the Viera y Clavijo Botanical Garden (Las Palmas de Gran Canaria), the Sóller Botanical Garden (Mallorca), the Santiago de Compostela Germplasm Bank and the National Centre for Plant Genetic Resources. As a result, 90 % of crucifer accessions and 50 % of rare endemic species are duplicated. To these national duplicates are added others held in banks in Greece, Japan and the United Kingdom.

Recent rescue plan

The great collecting effort of BGV-UPM’s first three decades, the laboriousness of sealing seeds, and the perennial shortage of technical staff led to a large stock of accessions conserved under suboptimal conditions, awaiting final sealing. With support from the Millennium Seed Bank (MSB) at the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew, a plan has been launched to rescue these accessions: germination tests and, where seed is lacking, regenerated (F1) seed production. The rescued material is ultra-dried and sealed, with part of it kept as a duplicate at the MSB.

In vitro culture

A unit created for the conservation of recalcitrant species that cannot tolerate BGV-UPM’s standard ultra-drying treatment.

Conservation networks

Since its creation in 1993, BGV-UPM has been part of the Collections Network of the National Plant Genetic Resources Plan (code ES003), managed by the Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries and Food. Since 2002 it has been part of the Spanish Network of Wild Plant Germplasm Banks and Native Phytoresources (REDBAG). In 2024 it signed the agreement to join the Networked Forest and Wild Flora Germplasm Bank (Banco en Red), under the Ministry for Ecological Transition and Demographic Challenge.

Research lines in conservation

Until the first decade of this century, research focused on very-long-term conservation techniques, seed germination and viability, and collection representativeness — the most active established line, although its full application to the bank’s materials remains pending. Without entirely abandoning those traditional lines, emerging lines are being launched: the search for and incorporation of CWR with potential adaptations to hostile environments; evaluation of CWR seedlings (microgreens) of high nutritional value; study of the mechanisms by which CWR adapt to water stress; study of the chemoprotective effect against cancer of CWR from the Brassicaceae and Amaranthaceae families; the creation, through the FAIR germplasm project, of a community of germplasm banks; and the development of COLLAPP, a mobile application to facilitate the recording of environmental data associated with the sampled population.
