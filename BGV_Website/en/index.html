Safeguarding the seed of Iberia’s wild flora

The “César Gómez Campo” Plant Germplasm Bank conserves the germplasm of wild species for the very long term, holding one of the most diverse crucifer (Brassicaceae) collections in the world.

Welcome

Ex situ conservation in the service of science

The Plant Germplasm Bank of the Universidad Politécnica de Madrid is part of the Department of Biotechnology–Plant Biology. Its main objective is to contribute to the ex situ conservation of wild plant species.

It comprises a seed bank, an in vitro conservation unit and a field collections unit. The research activity of the Department of Plant Biology can be consulted in its research reports.

~10,000 seed accessions conserved

What we do

Lines of work

Seed conservation

Collection, processing, storage and viability testing of wild species’ seeds, with special attention to the endemic and threatened flora of the Iberian Peninsula.

Conservation in vitro

Maintenance of plant material under controlled conditions for species with recalcitrant seeds or seeds otherwise difficult to conserve.

Field collections

Preservation of living plants in gardens and specialised collections for long-term study and conservation.

Research and development

Projects in conservation biology, seed ecology and climate-change adaptation, including crop wild relatives.

Flagship collection

The crucifer family

BGV-UPM was born in 1966 from a small collection of crucifer (Brassicaceae) seeds assembled by professor César Gómez Campo. Today it conserves almost 1,000 species of the Brassicaceae family in more than 5,000 accessions — one of the most diverse crucifer collections in the world, with particular relevance for Brassica oleracea and its wild relatives.

An associated project

FLAIR-GG virtual platform

A federated network of germplasm banks based on FAIR principles, integrating passport data from multiple institutions to improve its management, documentation and use — using BGV-UPM as the model bank.

Discover FLAIR-GG
  • Explore the resources of the federated network from a single place, without centralising the data.
  • Run searches and predefined queries using international standards.
  • Enrich passport data by connecting to GBIF, EIDOS and other open sources.
