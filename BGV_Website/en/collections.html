What we conserve

Collections

Three collections, built up over more than six decades of collecting, make up BGV-UPM’s holdings.

~10,000 seed accessions
~3,500 different species
3 units: seed bank, in vitro and field
~1,000 species

Crucifers (Brassicaceae)

The bank’s founding collection, begun in 1966. Almost 1,000 species of the Brassicaceae family in more than 5,000 accessions, including the main wild relatives of Brassica oleracea. One of the most diverse crucifer collections in the world.

See the history of this collection →

~1,400 accessions

Iberian, Balearic and Macaronesian endemics

Built up from 1973 with Proyecto Artemis and nearly 100 collaborators. It covers 24 % of Spain’s threatened flora and has served as a backup for in situ conservation actions, as in the case of Diplotaxis siettiana.

See the history of this collection →

Mediterranean basin

Crop wild relatives (CWR)

Begun in the 1980s under the sponsorship of the IBPGR, it incorporates seeds of wild relatives of cultivated Brassica oleracea, collected in Greece, Italy, Turkey, France, the United Kingdom and the Iberian Peninsula, among other regions.

How the material is organised

Seeds, in vitro culture and field collections

The Germplasm Bank comprises a seed bank, an in vitro conservation unit — for species with recalcitrant seeds or seeds otherwise difficult to conserve — and a field collections unit, which preserves living plants in gardens and specialised collections.
